# CRDS Annotation Guidelines

**Document version**: 1.0
**Audience**: Paid contractor designers participating in the CRDS labelling campaign.

---

## 1. What you will do

You will receive a sequence of design scenarios. For each scenario you
must produce **one 5-colour palette** that, in your professional judgement,
best serves the scenario as described in the brief. You will work
independently — you must not consult the other annotators during the
labelling phase.

---

## 2. What each scenario contains

* **Scenario ID** — for tracking (do not need to modify).
* **Domain** — one of: web/UI, marketing poster, infographic, e-commerce.
* **Brief** — a short text describing the design task and intended use.
* **Audience descriptor** — one of seven categories indicating the
  primary audience (children, teens, young adults, general adults,
  older adults, professionals, mixed/accessibility-first).
* **Reference image** — optional. When present, treat it as an
  inspirational mood reference, not a strict constraint.

---

## 3. What you will deliver per scenario

A 5-colour palette in **either**:
- 5 RGB hex codes (e.g., `#0F2A44`), or
- 5 CIELAB triplets (D65 white).

We will compute the missing representation automatically.

You may use any tool to produce the palette (Adobe Color, Coolors,
hand-picked, sampled from a reference image, etc.). What matters is
that the final five colours reflect your considered professional
choice for the scenario.

---

## 4. Procedure (per scenario)

1. **Read the brief carefully**, including the audience descriptor.
2. **Examine the reference image** if one is provided.
3. **Decide on a design intent**. Ask yourself: what mood / message
   should this palette convey for the stated audience?
4. **Construct a 5-colour palette** that, in your professional opinion,
   best serves that intent.
5. **Verify accessibility** for audiences flagged "high-contrast" or
   "WCAG-AA": at least one colour pair must meet 4.5:1 luminance contrast.
6. **Submit** the palette via the provided spreadsheet / form.

You should expect each scenario to take 4–7 minutes.

---

## 5. Constraints on the palette

* Exactly **five** colours.
* No two colours may be visually identical (CIELAB Δ < 5 between any
  pair is too close).
* Colours must be in the sRGB gamut (otherwise they will not display
  correctly on most screens).
* Avoid pure white (`#FFFFFF`) and pure black (`#000000`) unless the
  scenario clearly demands them.

---

## 6. What you should *not* do

* Do **not** consult colleagues or other annotators about specific
  scenarios.
* Do **not** copy palettes from public palette galleries (Adobe Kuler,
  Coolors, ColourLovers) wholesale; you may be inspired but the final
  palette must be your own composition.
* Do **not** reuse a palette across multiple scenarios.
* Do **not** add or remove colours; the palette must contain exactly five.

---

## 7. Quality assurance

A subset of scenarios (~5%) are duplicates inserted to measure
intra-annotator consistency. Please answer them as if they were new.

---

## 8. Compensation and timeline

* Total scenarios: 3,200.
* Estimated total time per annotator: ~270 hours.
* Compensation: agreed standard professional contractor rate per hour,
  paid in 4 staged batches at completion of each domain.
* Timeline: 12 weeks from project start.

---

## 9. Confidentiality

The briefs and reference images may not be redistributed outside the
project. You will be assigned a stable annotator ID (A1, A2, A3) for
the duration of the project; your real name and contact details will
not appear in any released file.
